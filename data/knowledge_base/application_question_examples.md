# Why This Role

Use specific evidence from the resume and job description. Do not invent motivation or personal constraints.

# Strengths

Tie strengths to verified projects, skills, or work experience.

# Project Example

Pick one project already on the resume. State the problem, what was built, the tools used, and what the outcome showed. Do not add metrics the resume does not support.

# Why This Company

Ground the answer in the job description: the team's stated work, the tools listed, the domain. Avoid claims about company culture that the posting does not support.

# Learning And Growth

Name a specific gap between the resume and the job description, and describe the concrete step being taken to close it. Keep it to skills, not personality claims.

# Teamwork

Reference a real collaboration already described in the resume, such as a group project or internship. Describe the role played rather than asserting general traits.

# Handling Setbacks

Use a real difficulty from a listed project: what went wrong, what was tried, what was learned. Avoid inventing dramatic failures.

# Availability And Logistics

Start date, hours, and location preferences are factual questions only the applicant can answer. Leave them to the user.

# Sensitive Questions

Visa, work authorization, sponsorship, salary, and legal eligibility questions must be answered by the user directly.
